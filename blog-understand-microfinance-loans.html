Before You Borrow: Questions to Ask About a Microfinance Loan

A borrower-friendly guide to understanding loan costs, repayment schedules, fees, savings, collateral and affordability before signing.

A loan can help a person purchase stock, manage a productive expense or grow a business. It can also create pressure when the borrower does not fully understand the cost or repayment schedule. Before accepting a microfinance loan, take time to understand the agreement in plain language. A responsible decision begins with questions, not pressure.

Ask for the total amount you will repay

Do not look only at the amount offered or the advertised interest rate. Ask for the full repayment amount, the number of instalments, their dates and the amount of each instalment. Request a written schedule and check whether the figures match the agreement.

Understand every fee separately

Ask whether there are joining, application, registration, processing, insurance or other charges. Find out which fees are paid upfront and which are deducted from the loan. Ask whether any fee is refundable and under what conditions. Never rely on a verbal explanation when the written contract says something different.

Clarify the interest method

Interest may be calculated in different ways, including flat-rate or reducing-balance methods. Ask how the lender calculates it, what period it covers and whether early repayment changes the amount due. Request a worked example using the actual loan amount and term you are considering.

Check savings and collateral arrangements

If savings are required, ask whether they remain your funds, how they are recorded, when they can be accessed and what happens if a loan becomes overdue. If collateral is involved, understand the conditions under which it may be held or applied. Get these terms in writing before paying.

Test whether repayments are affordable

Compare instalments with realistic business or household cash flow. Allow for rent, stock replacement, food, transport, school costs and unexpected expenses. Do not assume every day will be a good sales day. A repayment plan should remain manageable when income is lower than expected.

Verify the lender and keep your documents

Confirm the institution's identity and the relevant regulatory status for the product and jurisdiction. Read the agreement, ask questions and keep copies of receipts and signed documents. Never send money to an individual's personal number merely because someone claims to represent a lender.

Final thought

Borrowing should be based on clear information and a realistic repayment plan. Understand the total cost, fees, interest method, savings or collateral terms and what happens if you pay late. If an explanation is unclear, pause and seek clarification before signing. This article is general financial education, not personal borrowing advice.
