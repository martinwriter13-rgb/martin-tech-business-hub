How to Use AI at Work Without Losing Accuracy or Trust

By Martin Tech & Business Hub · Practical guide

AI tools can help people draft text, organise ideas, summarise material and explore possible solutions. They can also produce confident but inaccurate answers. Using AI responsibly means treating it as an assistant, checking its output and keeping people accountable for decisions that affect customers, colleagues or the public.

Choose tasks where AI can assist

Good starting points include brainstorming a meeting agenda, outlining an article, drafting a routine message or turning notes into a checklist. These tasks still need human review, but the tool can help create a first version. Avoid using AI as the sole decision-maker for legal, medical, financial, employment or other high-impact matters.

Give clear instructions and context

Explain the intended audience, purpose, tone, format and relevant facts. If the task has constraints, state them directly. For example, ask for a short customer email that acknowledges a delayed delivery, avoids promising an unconfirmed date and tells the customer when an update will be provided. Better instructions make it easier to assess whether the response meets the need.

Verify facts and calculations

Check names, dates, prices, statistics, quotations, links and policy statements against reliable sources. Ask for sources when researching, then open and review those sources yourself. Recalculate important figures independently. Do not publish an AI-generated claim simply because it sounds polished or includes a citation.

Protect private and confidential information

Do not enter passwords, identity documents, private customer records, confidential contracts or sensitive business data into a tool unless its approved terms and safeguards allow that use. Remove identifying details when a general example is enough. Follow workplace policies and applicable privacy obligations.

Keep human review and accountability

A person should review messages before they are sent, check documents before they are shared and approve decisions within their authority. Make corrections when the tool misunderstands context. If AI was used in a way that matters to the audience or is required by a policy, be transparent about it.

Measure whether AI actually helps

Compare the time and quality of an AI-assisted task with your normal process. Check whether it reduces repetitive effort without increasing errors or creating extra review work. Keep the tasks where it adds value and change or stop workflows that do not. The goal is better work, not simply more AI use.
