How to Prepare a Professional CV for Online Job Applications

By Martin Tech & Business Hub · Practical guide

A CV gives an employer a quick view of your experience, skills and suitability for a role. Online applications may be screened quickly, so a clear structure and relevant evidence matter. A strong CV does not need exaggerated language or complicated design; it needs accurate information that is easy to understand.

Start with accurate contact information

Include your name, a professional email address, phone number with the correct country code and location at a level you are comfortable sharing. Add a professional portfolio or LinkedIn link if it is relevant and up to date. Do not include passwords, identity numbers or unnecessary personal details.

Write a focused professional summary

Use a few sentences to describe your relevant experience, strengths and the kind of work you are applying for. Tailor the summary to the vacancy rather than using one broad statement for every job. Avoid unsupported claims such as being an expert in tools you have never used.

Describe experience with evidence

For each role, include the employer, job title and dates. Use bullet points to explain responsibilities and meaningful outcomes. Where you have reliable figures, include them with enough context to make them understandable. Do not invent targets, sales results, team sizes or achievements. If a result cannot be verified, describe the work accurately without a number.

Match skills to the vacancy

Read the job advert and identify the skills that matter most. Include relevant experience such as customer communication, sales, record keeping, research, scheduling or software use only when it reflects your actual abilities. Use the employer's terminology naturally, but never copy requirements that you cannot meet.

Keep formatting simple and readable

Use clear headings, consistent dates and a readable font. Avoid text boxes, complex graphics and layouts that may be difficult for application systems to parse. Save the document in the file format requested by the employer, usually PDF or DOCX. Use a clear file name such as Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.

Review before submitting

Check spelling, dates, contact details and consistency between the CV and application form. Make sure the file opens and contains no tracked changes or comments. Follow instructions about subject lines, attachments and supporting documents. Keep a copy of the exact version you submitted so you can prepare for an interview.
